If you work in a pharmaceutical QC lab, you already know that a tablet needs to be tough enough to survive packaging, shipping, and handling — without crumbling before it reaches the patient. That's exactly what a **tablet hardness tester** is designed to measure.

## What is Tablet Hardness?

Tablet hardness, also called **crushing strength** or **breaking force**, is a measure of how much force a tablet can withstand before it fractures. It's one of the most critical quality parameters in solid dosage form manufacturing.

Too soft, and the tablet crumbles during coating, packaging, or transport. Too hard, and it may not disintegrate properly in the patient's stomach — affecting drug release. The right hardness ensures both physical integrity and therapeutic effectiveness.

## How Does a Tablet Hardness Tester Work?

A tablet hardness tester applies a controlled, increasing force to the flat face of a tablet until it breaks. The instrument records the exact force at the point of fracture — expressed in Newtons (N), Kiloponds (kP), or Strong Cobb units (Sc).

Results are stored automatically, making them compliant with **21 CFR Part 11** requirements for electronic records.

## Pharmacopoeial Standards for Hardness Testing

Tablet hardness testing is governed by internationally recognised standards:

* **USP (United States Pharmacopeia)**

   — provides guidelines on crushing strength test methodology
* **EP (European Pharmacopoeia) 2.9.8**

   — specifies resistance to crushing of tablets
* **IP (Indian Pharmacopoeia)**

   — aligns with USP/EP requirements for Indian manufacturers

## Manual vs. Automatic Tablet Hardness Testers

Choosing between a manual and automatic tester depends on your lab's throughput and compliance requirements.

* **Manual testers**

   (e.g., Monsanto type, Pfizer type) are compact, affordable, and suitable for small-scale or R&D labs.
* **Semi-automatic testers**

   measure hardness, thickness, and diameter in one step — ideal for mid-size QC labs.
* **Fully automatic testers**

   with carousels handle high-volume testing with full data logging, audit trails, and GMP compliance built in.

## Why Tablet Hardness Testing Cannot Be Skipped

Inadequate hardness testing leads to real consequences — batch rejections, failed coating processes, and worst of all, poor drug bioavailability. Regulatory agencies expect hardness data as part of in-process quality control documentation for every batch.
