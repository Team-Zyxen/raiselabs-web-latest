A tablet that looks perfect on the production line can still fail in the real world — chipping during packaging, crumbling in a blister pack, or turning to powder by the time it reaches a patient. **Tablet friability testing** is the quality check that catches these problems before they become costly mistakes.

## What is Friability?

Friability refers to the tendency of a tablet to chip, crumble, or break under mechanical stress — specifically the kind of stress that occurs during normal handling, packaging, and transport. A **friability tester** simulates this stress by tumbling tablets inside a rotating drum for a fixed number of revolutions, then measuring the weight loss.

## The USP Friability Test Method

The standard test method is defined in **USP <1216>** and **EP 2.9.7**. The procedure is straightforward:

1. Weigh the tablet sample (typically 6.5g or 20 tablets, whichever is less)
2. Load tablets into the drum of the friability tester
3. Run the drum at 

   **25 RPM for 4 minutes**

    (100 revolutions)
4. Remove, de-dust, and re-weigh the tablets
5. Calculate percentage weight loss

**Acceptance limit:** Weight loss should not exceed **1.0%** for most tablets. Chewable tablets may have higher limits defined in their individual monographs.

## Single, Double & Triple Drum Testers — Which Do You Need?

Friability testers come in different configurations depending on throughput:

* **Single drum**

   — tests one batch at a time. Best for small labs, R&D.
* **Double drum**

   — tests two batches simultaneously. Most popular for QC labs with moderate throughput.
* **Triple drum**

   — tests three batches at once. Ideal for high-volume manufacturing environments.

## Common Causes of High Friability

Understanding why friability fails helps you fix it at the formulation stage:

* Insufficient binder concentration in the granulation
* Over-drying during the granulation process
* Too low compression force during tableting
* Poor particle size distribution

Running a friability test early in the development phase — not just at final batch release — helps formulators identify and correct these issues before scale-up.

## Friability vs. Hardness — What's the Difference?

These two tests are often confused but measure different things. **Hardness** measures resistance to crushing (a direct breaking force). **Friability** measures surface and edge durability under tumbling — a more realistic simulation of handling. Both tests are required in regulated manufacturing and complement each other well.
